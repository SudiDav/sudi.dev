## A personal journey

I was sitting across from a colleague, trying to explain Object-Oriented Programming (OOP), when something became clear to me. The confusion on their face was not only about the concept. Part of it was the language. I have spent years pushing myself to get better as a software developer, and along the way I found a tool that matters as much as any programming language or framework I have learned. That tool is **the English language itself**.

While I broke down terms like "polymorphism" and "encapsulation," I saw how much my understanding of English had shaped my grasp of these ideas. It made me wonder how many other developers are struggling with the language that describes the code, and not only with the code.

So in this post I want to share why English proficiency matters so much for a software developer, and why improving it may help you more than learning another framework.

## English is everywhere in programming

Most of software development happens in English, from the keywords of programming languages to documentation and online resources. Consider these points:

1.  **Programming languages.** Most programming languages use English keywords such as `if`, `while`, `return`, and `class`. If you know what those words mean in English, you already know a lot about what they do.

2.  **Documentation.** Official documentation, Stack Overflow answers, and GitHub repositories are mostly written in English. It is **the lingua franca, the bridge language,** of the programming world.

3.  **Collaboration.** International teams and open-source projects use English as their shared language.

4.  **Technical concepts.** Many programming concepts and design patterns have English names, and those names often describe the idea.

## English as a way into the concepts

A solid grasp of English makes many programming concepts easier to understand and remember. Here are a few examples from OOP.

### 1. Polymorphism

The word comes from Greek and means "many forms." That definition already explains the programming concept, which is a single interface for entities of different types.

![](/images/blog/a0a136e5f2a0.png)

Imagine a waiter in a restaurant. The waiter takes orders for different kinds of food, such as pizza, salad, or steak. Each dish is prepared differently, but the waiter uses the same "Prepare" command for all of them. That is polymorphism. One command ("Prepare") behaves differently depending on what it is applied to.

In code, you might have different types of employees (full-time, part-time, contractor) that all have a "calculate pay" method. Each type implements it according to its own payment structure.

### 2. Encapsulation

In English, to encapsulate means to enclose something in a capsule. In OOP, it means bundling data and the methods that operate on that data into a single unit.

![](/images/blog/adb156eccce7.png)

Think of a vending machine. You use it through a simple interface. You insert coins, press a button, and collect your item. You never see or touch its internal mechanism. The machine encapsulates (hides) its inner workings and exposes only what you need to use it.

In software, this is like a "Customer" class that keeps its data (name, address, and so on) private and only allows access through specific methods. This protects the data and makes the class simpler to use.

### 3. Inheritance

The everyday meaning, traits passed down from parents to children, matches its use in OOP, where child classes inherit properties and methods from parent classes.

![](/images/blog/d057f82ab038.png)

Consider a family tree. Children inherit traits from their parents, who inherited traits from their own parents. In programming, a "Vehicle" class passes basic properties down to more specific classes like "Car" or "Motorcycle".

This lets you reuse code and build hierarchies of related objects, the same way traits pass down through generations.

### 4. Abstraction

Abstraction means simplifying a complex system by modeling only the properties and behaviors that matter. In everyday English, "abstraction" means dealing with ideas rather than events. Knowing that meaning makes the programming use easier to grasp, which is hiding implementation details and exposing only the parts you need.

![](/images/blog/14fe9bc0015d.png)

Think about a car's dashboard. It gives you a simple interface to the complex systems of the car. You see the speed, the fuel level, and the warning lights, but you do not need to understand the mechanics behind them to drive. That is abstraction. You get the information you need, and the details stay hidden.

In programming, abstraction might be a "Shape" class with a "calculate area" method that each shape (circle, square, triangle) implements in its own way. The code that uses these shapes does not need to know the formulas. It only needs to know it can call "calculate area" on any shape.

## Other advantages of English proficiency

1.  **Naming.** Developers with strong English tend to write clearer variable names, function names, and comments. `calculateMonthlyInvoiceTotal` tells the next reader more than `calcInv`.

2.  **Learning speed.** Most resources are written in English, so being comfortable with it lets you learn new technologies and concepts faster.

3.  **Communication.** Software is built by teams, so you need to explain ideas, problems, and solutions clearly.

4.  **Culture.** Many programming jokes, memes, and cultural references are in English. Understanding them helps you feel part of the developer community.

## How to improve

If you want to improve your English as a developer, here are a few ideas:

1.  **Read widely.** Technical blogs, documentation, and non-technical English writing all build your vocabulary.

2.  **Join English-speaking developer communities.** Take part in forums, attend international conferences, or contribute to open-source projects.

3.  **Practice technical writing.** Start a blog or contribute to documentation projects. Explaining a concept in writing shows you quickly where your vocabulary runs out.

4.  **Pair program.** If you can, pair with native English speakers to improve your coding and your language skills at the same time.

## Conclusion

Coding skills come first, but English proficiency matters more in software development than many people admit. It helps you communicate, and it also helps you understand the concepts themselves. Time spent improving your English pays off in clearer code, better collaboration, and a deeper understanding of the craft.

As you keep growing as a developer, don't underestimate what better English can do for you.

And to my fellow developers who are struggling with the language barrier, keep pushing and keep learning. Every word you learn makes the documentation, the code, and the conversations around it a little easier to follow.
