Someone you care about replies to your message with one word.

*“Okay.”*

That is all they said. But you read it again. You check what you sent before it. You consider whether the full stop was necessary. 😂 By the time you put your phone down, you have had an entire argument with someone who might just be making dinner.

I have been thinking about how much can happen between reading that message and deciding what it means. On my homepage, there are five little planets around the words **I am**. I named them Emotion, Energy, Vibration, Frequency, and Reality. Looking at that scene, I keep coming back to the same question. How much of who I think I am comes from what I do in moments like this?

### What did I actually feel?

The first thing I have to admit is that the message bothered me. It is tempting to go straight to *“They are being rude”* because then I get to be right about something. Saying *“I felt hurt when I read that”* leaves me with a little more work to do. Why did it hurt? Did I want reassurance? Was I already worried about how things were between us?

That is what **emotion** does for me. It points to something worth paying attention to. Sometimes the feeling is about the person in front of me. Sometimes it has more to do with what I brought into the conversation that day. I usually need a minute to tell the difference.

Then there is **energy**. I can use the next hour to write a reply, delete it, and write a slightly more impressive version of the same bad reply. Or I can ask, *“Hey, are we okay?”* I might even put my phone down until I can ask it without sounding like I already have witnesses lined up. 😂

### The same words can land differently

Say *“What happened?”* as if you really want to know. Then say it as if you have already decided whose fault it is. A transcript would look the same. The person hearing you knows the difference.

That is how I think about **vibration**. Some of what I feel comes through before I get to explain myself. It is in the way I ask the question, how long I let someone speak, and whether I am listening or getting my answer ready.

I like that the yin-yang is in the middle of the scene. It gives me a way to think about two things that can be true in the same moment. I can be angry and still care about the person I am angry with. I can pray for patience in the morning and discover by lunchtime that I have somewhere to practise it.

### What happens when I keep doing it?

If I get defensive once, we had a difficult conversation. If I do it every time someone brings me bad news, people eventually learn to bring me less bad news. Then I get to walk around thinking things are fine. That is a very convenient arrangement for me, right up until something breaks.

Think about a team where reporting a problem always turns into a meeting about whose fault it was. After a while, you hear about problems later. The meeting itself may have lasted twenty minutes. What matters is that everybody knows what happens every time.

That is **frequency** as I understand it. How often do I return to a particular response? I can practise asking one more question before I defend myself. And when I get it wrong, I can come back and say, *“I heard the first half of what you said and started arguing with the second half in my head. Can we try again?”*

### Let the other person answer

**Reality** gets a turn when I finally ask the question and listen to the answer. Maybe they were making dinner. Maybe they really were upset and have been trying to tell me for a week. I have to live with what they actually say, including the possibility that I will have something to change on my side.

There are parts of reality I had no hand in choosing. Other people have their own choices to make. What I do next is still worth thinking about.

And that brings me back to **I am**. How quickly does *“I feel embarrassed”* become *“I am bad at this”*? How many times have I said *“That is just how I am”* about something I could still learn to do differently?

I think I want to leave a little room after those two words. Today I might be able to say, *“I am learning to listen.”* Then somebody will send me a message, and I will get another chance to find out what that looks like.
