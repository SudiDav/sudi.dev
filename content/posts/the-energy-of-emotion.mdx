There are five little planets on my homepage. I gave them names we tend to use when we’re trying to understand what’s going on inside us and around us. Energy. Emotion. Reality. Vibration. Frequency. And in the middle of it all are two words I use every day without thinking much about them.

**I am.**

I am tired. I am afraid. I am someone who always gives up. Those sentences start the same way, but the first two describe what’s happening right now. The third sounds like a decision about the rest of my life. When did I make that decision? Maybe I just said it often enough that it started to sound like a fact.

### Begin with what you feel

Imagine getting a short reply from someone you care about. Just *“Okay.”* You read it again. Then you open it a third time, as if the punctuation might have changed. 😂 Before you know it, the person is angry with you and you’re having an argument with them in your head. They might just be in the middle of making dinner.

**Emotion** tells me that something matters. In that moment, I might feel hurt because I care what this person thinks, or afraid because I’ve been misunderstood before. There’s something worth listening to there. I can give the feeling a name and still leave room for the other person to tell me what actually happened.

### Give the feeling somewhere to go

That’s where **energy** comes in for me. It takes energy to replay a conversation ten times. It also takes energy to ask, *“Hey, are we okay?”* The second one might cost me ten seconds and a little pride. The first can take up an afternoon.

Sometimes the right direction is a conversation. Sometimes I need to put the phone down, go for a walk, or sleep before I decide what that message means. I can be upset and still choose what to do with the next five minutes.

### Notice what you bring into the room

I use **vibration** to talk about the tone that travels with us. Think about “What happened?” spoken with curiosity, then hear it again with suspicion. Same words. The person across from you will probably give two very different answers.

This is why I like having the yin-yang in the middle of the picture. I can be angry and still care about the person I’m talking to. I can be afraid and still ask an honest question. If I make room for both, I might hear something I would have missed while I was busy building my case.

### Pay attention to what you repeat

**Frequency** is how often I return to a way of living. Answer defensively once and you’ve had a difficult conversation. Do it often enough and someone who loves you may start rehearsing what they want to say before they come to you.

Repetition can work the other way, too. If I regularly ask a question before assuming the worst, people start to learn that they can explain themselves to me. And on the days when I get it wrong, I can practise coming back and saying, *“I was already answering you before you finished. Can we try that again?”*

### Let reality answer back

**Reality** is what happens when other people and circumstances enter the picture. A gentle question can open a conversation. It can also reveal that there really is a problem. Either way, I’ve learned something I couldn’t learn by rereading *“Okay.”*

Life contains things we never chose. Other people have their own decisions to make. Even when I do everything I know how to do, the answer may still hurt. I can pay attention to what happened and decide what I’m able to do next.

That’s why I put *I am* in the middle. Some days I’m tired and impatient. On a good day I might call myself calm, right up until somebody sends me another one-word reply. 😂 I’m learning to leave a little space after those two words.

Maybe today all I can honestly say is, *“I am learning to listen.”* The next conversation will give me a chance to.
